Sources of the Ottawa data

This site is not the City of Ottawa and has no official status. If anything differs, the City’s own document prevails: it is one click away on every card.

Where the data comes from

  • Decisions and recorded votes: the minutes of City Council and its standing committees, published by the City Clerk on pub-ottawa.escribemeetings.com, read once per meeting, in English and in French. Nothing is translated by us.
  • Councillors, wards, attendance, contracts, budget, taxes by ward, construction, parks and permits: the City’s open data, under the Open Government Licence – City of Ottawa, version 2.0. The data sets are read through ArcGIS services, not through the open.ottawa.ca portal, whose robots.txt asks for 60 seconds between requests.
  • Our script identifies itself and waits at least two seconds between two requests. It does not read ottawa.ca. The links to the councillors’ pages and the terms of use lead there without being read.

What a machine does and what it can miss

  • The summaries are written by AI from the text of the minutes, item by item and identified as such. They can be wrong. The minutes prevail. Procedural items (adjournment, confirmation of minutes…) get no summary.
  • The “In brief” sentence of each meeting is also written by AI, from the list of its decisions. We corrected the current ones by hand after a review. The three highlighted items are picked without AI, by rule (summarized items first, then amounts, split votes and by-laws).
  • The subject tags (zoning, contracts, heritage…) do not exist in the minutes: they are added here by keywords on the English title of the item. A wrong tag is our mistake, not the City’s.
  • Names in recorded votes: the minutes write “M. Luloff”: we link the initial and the family name to the councillor’s full name. A name that matches nobody stays as the minutes write it.
  • Budget amounts: the City’s budget files do not name their unit. Their sum (operating 2026: 5,220,570) matches a budget of about $5.2 billion, so the amounts are read as thousands of dollars. That is a deduction, not an official statement.

Things to know

  • This is a preview. It covers the meetings of September 14 to November 15, 2026 and is not updated automatically yet.
  • The same file often appears twice: once in a standing committee, then again when Council makes it final. Both are real decisions and both are shown.
  • Most items carry without a recorded vote. When the minutes record dissent without a recorded vote, the names are shown as the minutes give them.
